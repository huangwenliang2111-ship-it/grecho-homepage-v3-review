Common problems

Common Fire Performance Problems

Fire performance depends on more than the fiberglass facer alone. The coating, adhesive, core and tested end-use configuration can all matter. Below are six customer issues for technical evaluation.

01

Failure to Meet Fire Classification Requirements

The specified fire classification may not be achieved when the facing, adhesives, core and installation configuration are evaluated together. A facer’s individual properties do not establish the classification of the finished product.

Explore solution →

02

Excessive Combustible Coating Content

High quantities of organic binder or other combustible coating ingredients can add fuel load to the facer and affect the reaction-to-fire behavior of the completed panel.

Explore solution →

03

Fire Performance Variation Between Batches

Changes in raw-material formulation, adhesive add-on or coating weight may create variability in reaction-to-fire results across production batches.

Explore solution →

04

Thermal Degradation of Facing Materials

At elevated service temperatures, coatings or adhesive layers can weaken, discolor or lose bond integrity, potentially compromising the facing or laminate. This is a thermal-durability issue and should not be confused with fire classification.

Explore solution →

05

Reduced Fire Performance After Lamination

A facing that performs acceptably by itself may produce different fire-test results after lamination to a core or application of additional adhesive layers.

Explore solution →

06

Insufficient Fire-Test Documentation

Missing reports, mismatched product specifications or unclear test scope can delay technical approval and make fire-performance claims difficult to substantiate.

Explore solution →

Targeted solutions

Targeted Solutions, Problem by Problem

The following recommended directions relate to the six issues discussed above. Any classification claim must be tied to applicable testing and the exact construction covered by the report.

01

Design for the Intended Classification

Customer problem

Failure to Meet Fire Classification Requirements

The specified fire classification may not be achieved when the facing, adhesives, core and installation configuration are evaluated together. A facer’s individual properties do not establish the classification of the finished product.

GRECHO solution

Review the fiberglass substrate, organic coating and adhesive load together, favoring lower-combustible-content options where feasible. Verify the actual finished board and intended installation configuration under the applicable classification scheme.

Related materials

White Acoustic Ceiling Facer

02

Reduce Combustible Coating Content

Customer problem

Excessive Combustible Coating Content

High quantities of organic binder or other combustible coating ingredients can add fuel load to the facer and affect the reaction-to-fire behavior of the completed panel.

GRECHO solution

Optimize the inorganic filler-to-organic-binder balance while retaining the required appearance, adhesion and processability. Confirm that the revised formulation continues to satisfy other essential product requirements.

Related materials

Matte Black Fiberglass Coated Veil

03

Strengthen Batch-to-Batch Process Control

Customer problem

Fire Performance Variation Between Batches

Changes in raw-material formulation, adhesive add-on or coating weight may create variability in reaction-to-fire results across production batches.

GRECHO solution

Define controls for formulation, adhesive quantity and coating weight, then carry out scheduled performance sampling and retain batch traceability records. Investigate deviations before release.

Related materials

Fiberglass Coated Veil for Gypsum Board

04

Specify for Service Temperature and Heat Aging

Customer problem

Thermal Degradation of Facing Materials

At elevated service temperatures, coatings or adhesive layers can weaken, discolor or lose bond integrity, potentially compromising the facing or laminate. This is a thermal-durability issue and should not be confused with fire classification.

GRECHO solution

Choose heat-resistant coating and adhesive systems based on the operating temperature and exposure duration. Assess structural integrity and bond retention after relevant heat-aging tests.

Related materials

Fiberglass Coated Veil for PIR PUR Insulation Boards

05

Evaluate the Complete Laminated System

Customer problem

Reduced Fire Performance After Lamination

A facing that performs acceptably by itself may produce different fire-test results after lamination to a core or application of additional adhesive layers.

GRECHO solution

Review the combustible contribution of the core and adhesive, optimize laminate construction where possible, and test the completed assembly in its intended end-use configuration.

Related materials

Washable Fiberglass Coated Veil

06

Build a Specification-Specific Test Dossier

Customer problem

Insufficient Fire-Test Documentation

Missing reports, mismatched product specifications or unclear test scope can delay technical approval and make fire-performance claims difficult to substantiate.

GRECHO solution

Maintain test reports connected to the exact product construction and intended application, together with relevant batch documentation, specification identifiers and clearly stated test or classification scope.

Fiberglass Coated Veil for Mineral Wool Roof Insulation

Application scenarios

Application Scenarios

The following are illustrative end-use contexts for the associated facers. Test standards and classes should be selected for each target market and finished system.

Acoustic Ceiling & Wall Panels

Assess the complete panel under the applicable reaction-to-fire test and evaluate whether color, matte finish, coating load and cleaning performance affect results.

Gypsum Board Systems

Confirm the performance of the specific gypsum-board construction, including the fiberglass facing and attachment method, rather than relying on the facing alone.

PIR/PUR Foam Insulation Boards

Account for the core, facing and lamination adhesive in system testing; independently verify any heat-aging or long-term adhesion requirements.

Mineral Wool Roof Insulation

Match facing and adhesive documentation to the final insulation assembly and intended roof application, noting any installation conditions covered by test reports.
