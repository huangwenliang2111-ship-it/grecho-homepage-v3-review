01 / Common problems

Common Acoustic Performance Problems

The facing is part of an acoustic system—not an isolated decorative layer. Its pore structure, coating, and processing can influence how sound interacts with the underlying core. The following six challenges come from the supplied Acoustic Performance table.

Challenge 01

Insufficient Air Permeability

When a facer does not allow sufficient airflow into the porous acoustic core, the finished panel may not achieve its intended sound absorption performance.

Explore solution →

Challenge 02

Excessive Airflow Resistance

An overly restrictive facing layer can limit the movement of air through the panel surface, making it harder to tune the overall acoustic behavior of the system.

Explore solution →

Challenge 03

Inconsistent Acoustic Performance

Variation in facer permeability or airflow resistance across rolls and batches can contribute to inconsistent acoustic results in finished panels.

Explore solution →

Challenge 04

Imbalance Between Surface Coverage and Acoustic Performance

Greater coating coverage may improve visual hiding, yet excessive pore blockage can reduce the airflow needed by certain sound-absorbing constructions.

Explore solution →

Challenge 05

Inadequate Sound Reflection Control

Spaces designed for speech clarity may require a controlled degree of sound reflection rather than maximum absorption. An unsuitable facing can disrupt that balance.

Explore solution →

Challenge 06

Acoustic Performance Loss After Lamination

Even if an individual facer meets its initial airflow target, adhesive application during lamination may obstruct pores and reduce acoustic performance after assembly.

Explore solution →

04 / Application scenarios

Application Scenarios

Sound-Absorbing Acoustic Ceiling Panels

In offices, learning spaces, and other interiors requiring sound absorption, a suitably breathable facer can help expose the porous core to incident sound.

Acoustic Wall Panels

Surface airflow characteristics, fabric structure, and the wall panel core should be evaluated together to meet the specified acoustic performance.

Speech-Oriented Lecture Halls & Conference Rooms

Some acoustic designs need a balance between absorption and sound reflection to support audibility across a larger space; facing selection should follow the overall acoustic design.

Laminated Acoustic Composite Panels

For factory-laminated ceiling and wall products, adhesive application and post-bonding acoustic tests help ensure that the final assembly performs as intended.
