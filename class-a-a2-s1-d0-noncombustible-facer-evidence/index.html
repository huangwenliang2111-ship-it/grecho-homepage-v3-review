Class A, A2-s1,d0 and “Noncombustible”: Which Question Does Each Actually Answer?

An ASTM E84-based Class A surface-burning classification, an EN 13501-1 A2-s1,d0 reaction-to-fire classification and a noncombustibility statement are not interchangeable labels. Read the method, classified product, specimen construction and application scope before accepting the claim. None of these expressions, by itself, gives an unspecified finished wall or ceiling a fire-resistance period.

A fire-document review can go wrong before the report is opened. One quotation says “Class A”; another says “A2-s1,d0”; a third says “noncombustible”. The buyer asks which is better, assuming that the three phrases sit on one common scale.

They do not. The useful first question is which requirement the project actually specifies. Comparing a surface-burning result with a different classification route is not a shortcut to compliance; it is a reason to read the evidence more carefully.

This is a document-review guide, not a certification statement about any GRECHO material or a determination of the rules applying to a particular building.

What does A2-s1,d0 mean?

A2-s1,d0 is a reaction-to-fire classification, not a fire-resistance rating in minutes. A2 identifies the main reaction-to-fire class; s1 identifies the smoke-production class; d0 identifies the class for flaming droplets or particles. The designation does not, by itself, establish 30, 60 or 120 minutes of fire resistance for a wall, ceiling or other building element.

For a fiberglass facer review, keep the complete designation attached to the identified product or construction. Do not shorten it to “A2” and lose the additional classifications, or turn the numeral 2 into a duration.

Part of the designation What it identifies What it does not establish
A2 The main Euroclass reaction-to-fire category, subject to its specified test and classification criteria. Two hours of fire resistance, an A1 classification, or unrestricted suitability for every construction.
s1 A smoke-production class based on smoke-growth and total-smoke criteria. Zero smoke or a smoke-toxicity assessment.
d0 No flaming droplets or particles in the EN 13823 single burning item test within 600 seconds. A fire-resistance duration or a guarantee of no falling material in every fire scenario.

This decoding concerns the construction-product category excluding floorings, linear pipe thermal insulation and electric cables. Those categories have their own classification provisions. See the EU reaction-to-fire classification framework.

Does A2-s1,d0 give a fire-rating time?

No. A2-s1,d0 answers a reaction-to-fire question. A requirement expressed as a period of fire resistance asks a different question about an identified building element and the functions it must maintain.

EN 13501-1 concerns classification from reaction-to-fire tests and considers products in their practical application. EN 13501-2 concerns classification using fire-resistance and/or smoke-control test data for the elements within its scope. One designation should not be converted into the other.

The observation period used by a reaction-to-fire test is also not a fire-resistance rating. A test can observe a particular behaviour for a stated period without proving that a wall or ceiling maintains its required functions for that period.

Hypothetical purchasing example: a request says “A2-s1,d0 and 60-minute fire resistance”. Treat this as two requirements to clarify, not two ways of expressing the same result. Ask which product needs the reaction-to-fire classification and which building element, assembly and assessment criteria the 60-minute requirement concerns. This example is not a GRECHO performance claim.

Why “Class A” is not a shortcut to A2-s1,d0

Always identify the classification system behind “Class A”. When the reference is ASTM E84, the evidence concerns comparative surface-burning behaviour. ASTM explicitly states that a flame-spread index alone does not classify a material as noncombustible. It also states that the method does not measure heat transmission through the tested surface.

Consequently, an ASTM E84-based Class A statement is not a substitute for an EN 13501-1 classification or for a building-element fire-resistance result. Keep the methods, reports and product identities separate. See the ASTM E84 scope and limitations.

Write the test question beside the headline

The table is a guide to different questions, not a conversion chart. ASTM E84, EN 13501-1 and fire-resistance testing address distinct evidence routes.[1][2][5]

ASTM E84 does not turn a flame-spread index into noncombustibility

ASTM E84 reports comparative surface-burning behaviour through flame-spread and smoke-developed indices. Its published scope expressly excludes defining a material as noncombustible from a flame-spread index alone.[1]

For a purchaser, the immediate action is to obtain the actual indices and the classification basis, rather than retain only “Class A” from a sales summary. Identify the report edition, specimen description, exposed face and mounting or support arrangements.

This matters for facings because the support is not just a detail in the photograph. ASTM's public scope notes that support and specimen behaviour can affect the relationship between reported results.[1] Do not delete the mounting description from a statement and then treat the remaining class as independent of construction.

A result is not weakened by accurately stating its scope. It becomes more useful because the buyer can compare it with the intended application.

A2-s1,d0 is a complete classification expression, not three promises

EN 13501-1 provides a reaction-to-fire classification procedure for relevant construction products.[2] In A2-s1,d0, A2 is the main class, while the suffixes concern smoke production and flaming droplets or particles under the applicable assessment.

The European classification criteria make s1 a defined smoke category, not a statement of zero smoke or harmless combustion gases. Similarly, d0 concerns the specified absence of flaming droplets or particles during the defined test observation, not a promise about every possible fire.[3]

Keep the full classification with the identified product and application scope. Do not translate it into “100% fireproof”, “smoke-free” or an ASTM-based class. The systems do not become interchangeable because their headline labels contain the letter A.

The relevant report may describe substrates, fixing, joints, thicknesses or other construction conditions. Ask the issuer or competent assessment body how those conditions relate to the intended product. A copied first-page classification without its field of application can leave that question unanswered.

A noncombustibility request needs a suitable test route

ASTM E136 is an example of a method addressing combustibility under specified furnace conditions. Its public scope says that passing materials are typically classified as noncombustible, while also stating that the method does not apply to laminated or coated materials.[4]

That limitation is especially important when discussing coated or composite facers. “Arrange E136 for everything” is not a reliable response to a noncombustibility request. The material form, relevant requirement and suitable route need to be established with the competent laboratory or assessment body.

Nor does a statement about a glass constituent automatically establish the status of a finished coated facing or a board containing it. Ask what product was assessed. Do not silently switch the subject from fibre to facer to finished assembly halfway through the claim.

A correct class can still be quoted incorrectly

Consider this hypothetical purchasing review. A report covers an identified facing under stated mounting conditions. A brochure then describes the facing as suitable for any board system and uses the result to imply that the board is noncombustible.

No change to the original test result is needed for the brochure to become misleading. The problem is the transfer: a result for one described object has been extended to another object and a different claim. This is a review example, not a description of a GRECHO customer or report.

A safer statement identifies the result and its scope, then makes the remaining question explicit: whether the intended board and installation are covered. If not, obtain an appropriate assessment or test rather than writing a stronger sentence.

Fire resistance is a separate boundary. ASTM E119, for example, addresses fire testing of defined building constructions and materials; it does not allow a generic facer class to become an hourly rating for an unspecified assembly.[5]

Check the document against the question you are asking

Use the following checklist to turn a search phrase into a specific document-review question. It is a purchasing aid, not a classification procedure or a statement that a product is suitable for a particular building.

Search or purchasing phrase Question to resolve Document detail to verify
“A2-s1,d0 fire rating” Which product or construction has that classification? Exact designation, product identification, classification document and stated application scope.
“A2-s1,d0 fire rating time” Is the requirement actually for fire resistance of a building element? The applicable element/assembly evidence, its classification and the associated period, where required.
“Noncombustible facer” What definition and evidence does the specification require? Named test/classification system and the product to which the claim applies; not a marketing label alone.
“A2-s1,d0 board with this facer” Does the available evidence cover the proposed faced construction? Facing, core, bonding, finish, mounting and application details described in the evidence.

Do not assign a finished-board result to a loose facer, or assume that a facer result covers every board on which it may be used. Where the construction differs or is insufficiently described, record the open scope question for the responsible technical reviewer.

For the wider construction-matching workflow, use Why a Facer Test Report Does Not Automatically Prove Finished-Board Performance. For choosing the document pack, use our TDS, SDS and test-report request guide.

What GRECHO can help clarify

In a facer enquiry, provide the exact project requirement and intended board construction. GRECHO can coordinate available technical documentation and the material direction to be reviewed. Where application of a classification remains uncertain, the report issuer, qualified laboratory or responsible project professional should confirm it.

Selected evidence may be relevant without being suitable for unrestricted publication. Use the technical-document request process rather than treating a request for clarification as permission to circulate an entire report.

A useful review ends with a precise sentence: what has been demonstrated, for which product, under which conditions, and which decision remains open. “Class A, therefore safe for everything” is not that sentence.

Questions buyers ask about fire labels

Is Class A equivalent to A2-s1,d0?

No direct equivalence should be assumed. The labels belong to different assessment and classification systems. Check the requirement, test methods, classified product and application scope rather than converting one label into the other.

Can an ASTM E84 result prove that a coated facer is noncombustible?

A flame-spread index by itself cannot establish noncombustibility. The appropriate assessment depends on the material and the applicable requirement; ASTM E136 also has material-scope limitations that must not be ignored.

Does a classified facer make the complete board fire-rated?

Not automatically. Determine whether the evidence covers the board construction and intended application. Reaction-to-fire classification, noncombustibility and assembly fire resistance should remain distinct claims.

Need to clarify the facer evidence for a board application? Share the selected facer direction, intended board construction, target market and exact fire-performance requirement. State whether the question concerns reaction-to-fire classification or fire resistance in minutes.
