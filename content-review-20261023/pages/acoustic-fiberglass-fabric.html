Woven for Design. Engineered for Acoustic Spaces.

SURFACE DESIGN MATTERS

What Makes a Quality Acoustic Fabric Surface?

Review the surface and the production process together before selecting a fabric.

01

Consistent texture

Check yarn arrangement across adjoining panels; weave variation can remain visible after installation.

02

Suitable appearance

Compare how each weave changes texture and light reflection in the intended interior.

03

Acoustic compatibility

Fabric density and treatment can change airflow resistance and finished-panel sound absorption.

04

Production stability

Assess tension control, lamination and cutting with the intended woven structure.

Surface design

Panel compatibility

THREE WEAVES. DISTINCTIVE TEXTURES.

Choose the Texture That Defines Your Acoustic Panel

Use samples to compare texture and visual character. The same facing must also suit the acoustic construction and production method.

01

Plain weave

A regular over-and-under structure gives a balanced, uniform texture. Consider it for offices, classrooms and general ceiling or wall panels with an understated finish.

02

Satin weave

Longer yarn floats and fewer visible interlacing points give a smoother-looking surface with subtle directional texture. Consider it for hotels, conference facilities and architectural panels.

03

Jacquard weave

Patterned weaving creates more complex decorative textures. Consider it for hospitality interiors, auditoriums and wall panels requiring a recognizable woven design.

HOW IT WORKS

A Woven Surface. An Essential Acoustic Layer.

The fabric forms the visible decorative layer. Mineral wool or glass wool supplies the primary sound-absorbing structure of the finished panel.

Woven fiberglass facing

Plain, satin or jacquard construction provides the surface texture, with airflow characteristics dependent on weave and treatment.

Mineral wool or glass wool core

Evaluate the fabric, coating, adhesive and core as a complete panel to balance appearance, adhesion and sound absorption.

FAQ

Questions Manufacturers Ask Before Choosing Acoustic Fiberglass Fabric

Can fabric change sound absorption?

Yes. Weave density, airflow resistance and treatment can affect finished-panel performance. Select and test the fabric with the sound-absorbing core.

What can be customized?

Color, weave, width, basis weight and surface treatment may be available, subject to product grade and manufacturing feasibility.

Can the same grade be used on ceilings and walls?

Both applications can be evaluated. Grade selection depends on panel construction, installation, surface requirements and applicable performance standards.

What fire classification applies?

Fiberglass is an inorganic, noncombustible fiber, but the fire performance of a finished fabric or acoustic panel also depends on coatings, binders, adhesives, and other components. Applicable fire classification should be confirmed through product-specific testing.
