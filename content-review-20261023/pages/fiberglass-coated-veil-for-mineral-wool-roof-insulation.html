02 — ROOF INSULATION CHALLENGES

Roof insulation challenges

Temperature, humidity and incidental wetting place different demands on the facing and roof assembly.

01 — Surface wetting

Condensation, installation moisture or accidental water exposure can introduce moisture at the insulation surface.

02 — Restricted drying

Unsuitable vapor control can trap moisture and reduce the intended drying potential of the roof construction.

03 — Fiber disturbance

Transport, handling and installation can abrade or damage an unprotected mineral wool surface.

04 — Facing delamination

Adhesive, coating and process incompatibility can weaken the facing-to-core bond.

03 — PROTECT THE SURFACE AND PRESERVE DRYING REQUIREMENTS

Protect the surface and preserve drying requirements

The coated fiberglass structure provides a consistent surface for compatible mineral wool boards. Select the coating and substrate for the intended roof construction, lamination method and handling conditions.

01 — Liquid-water resistance

Selected coatings help reduce direct liquid water penetration through the facing under specified conditions.

02 — Moisture management

Evaluate water resistance and vapor permeability independently against the roof assembly's requirements.

03 — Lamination and handling

Check coating, substrate and adhesive compatibility for surface protection, dimensional stability and consistent conversion.

04 — FACING OPTIONS FOR PITCHED ROOFS

Facing options for pitched roofs

Choose the facing according to water exposure, drying strategy, mineral wool core and production requirements.

01 — Standard coated veil

A uniform surface for conventional mineral wool board production where handling stability, appearance and consistent processing are the priorities.

02 — Moisture-resistant coated veil

A selected coating can reduce liquid water penetration through the exposed facing. Evaluate it for boards needing additional protection against incidental moisture exposure.

03 — Vapor-permeable coated veil

Selectable vapor transmission supports evaluation of constructions designed for drying. Confirm the grade with the complete pitched roof assembly rather than selecting permeability alone.

06 — FACING AND MINERAL WOOL CORE

Facing and mineral wool core

The facing forms a protective surface layer. Mineral wool provides the board's primary thermal insulation function and may contribute to roof-assembly acoustic and fire performance.

01 — Coated fiberglass veil

A lightweight substrate with selectable coating properties for water resistance, vapor permeability and core compatibility.

02 — Mineral wool insulation core

Assess facing and core as a complete board for adhesive bonding, dimensional stability, moisture behavior and relevant finished-board performance.

11 — MOISTURE AND ROOF-SYSTEM QUESTIONS

Moisture and roof-system questions

Q1 — Does the facing make the roof moisture-proof?

Selected grades can reduce direct liquid water penetration. Finished-system moisture performance also depends on the core, joints, membranes and roof design.

Q2 — Can the facing guarantee mold prevention?

Moisture-resistant facing may help limit certain forms of surface wetting. However, mold prevention depends on the complete roof assembly, including moisture control, condensation management, ventilation, and adjacent materials. The facing alone cannot guarantee mold prevention.

Q3 — Is lower vapor transmission always better?

Not necessarily. A roof needs an appropriate balance of water resistance and vapor management. Restricting transmission may be unsuitable where it limits intended drying.

Q4 — Can the same grade suit different roof configurations?

Evaluate suitability for the board design, installation method, moisture strategy and applicable building requirements. Coating, basis weight and width can be discussed for the core and process.
