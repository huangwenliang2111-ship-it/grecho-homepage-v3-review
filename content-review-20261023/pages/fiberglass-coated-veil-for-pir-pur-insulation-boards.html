02 — PRODUCTION CHALLENGES TO CHECK

Production challenges to check

Facing properties must work with expanding foam and continuous processing conditions.

01 — Foam adhesion

Incompatible coating or surface characteristics can weaken the bond to the PIR/PUR core.

02 — Flatness and tension

Deformation or inconsistent tension can cause wrinkles, distortion and surface-flatness difficulties.

03 — Reinforcement direction

Some panel designs need directional or multidirectional support beyond a standard facing structure.

04 — Continuous processing

Variation in thickness, coating or strength can affect unwinding, foam application and lamination.

03 — MATCH THE FACING TO FOAM AND LINE CONDITIONS

Match the facing to foam and line conditions

Select surface coating and fiberglass structure against the actual foam formulation and bonding requirements. Consistent geometry and material characteristics support handling and integration with continuous board production.

01 — Surface and substrate

Evaluate coating compatibility and substrate stability through unwinding, foaming and lamination.

02 — Reinforcement where required

Choose yarn or mesh according to the required tensile direction and processing conditions, with grade-specific evaluation.

04 — THREE FACING STRUCTURES

Three facing structures

Compare reinforcement direction as well as surface characteristics. The correct structure depends on panel design and production needs.

01 — Standard coated veil

A uniform coated fiberglass facing without additional yarn or mesh. Consider it for conventional PIR/PUR boards requiring surface consistency, stable processing and suitable foam compatibility.

02 — Yarn-reinforced coated veil

Additional fiberglass yarns provide targeted tensile support along the reinforcement direction. Evaluate this option where handling or production requires added support in a selected direction.

03 — Mesh-reinforced coated veil

Fiberglass mesh adds tensile support in both longitudinal and transverse directions. Evaluate it for panel designs needing reinforcement in both principal directions and additional handling stability.

06 — FACING AND FOAM CORE

Facing and foam core

The coated veil supplies the facing surface and optional reinforcement. The rigid polyisocyanurate or polyurethane core provides the finished panel's primary thermal insulation function.

01 — Coated fiberglass facing

A lightweight veil with engineered coating; the selected grade may incorporate yarn or mesh reinforcement.

02 — PIR/PUR foam core

Evaluate the complete panel for bonding strength, dimensional stability, thermal performance and manufacturing compatibility.

11 — QUESTIONS BEFORE SPECIFYING

Questions before specifying

Q1 — Can the facing work with both PIR and PUR?

Both systems can be evaluated. Final suitability depends on foam formulation, processing conditions and bonding performance.

Q2 — Does reinforcement increase facing strength?

Yarn or mesh can improve selected tensile properties and handling stability. Actual improvement depends on design, material weight, bonding structure and product grade.

Q3 — Does a reinforced facing guarantee panel strength?

Reinforced facing may contribute to selected mechanical characteristics of the finished panel. However, overall panel strength also depends on foam density, core structure, facing adhesion, panel dimensions, and other construction factors.

Q4 — What can be customized?

Yarn spacing, mesh configuration, basis weight, roll width and coating may be available, subject to grade and manufacturing feasibility.
