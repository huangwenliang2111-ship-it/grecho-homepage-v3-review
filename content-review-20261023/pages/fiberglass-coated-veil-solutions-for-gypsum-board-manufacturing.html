Gypsum board applications

Choose the facer for the board type and service conditions.

01 — Interior boards

Review surface consistency, core bonding and dimensional stability for interior walls and ceilings.

02 — Exterior sheathing

Consider moisture and weather exposure. Selected UV-resistant facer options require application-specific review.

03 — Moisture-resistant boards

Review the water-resistant coating for the moisture-prone environment and intended board construction.

04 — Fire-resistant boards

Select the facing within a gypsum board system designed for the specified fire-performance requirements.

03 — FIND THE CAUSE OF THE BOARD DEFECT

Find the cause of the board defect

Assess the surface and core together with the slurry, board forming process and drying conditions.

01

01 — Insufficient slurry penetration

An excessively dense coating or low permeability can limit penetration into the fiberglass and weaken mechanical interlocking. Review slurry interaction before treating a bonding problem as an adhesive issue alone.

02

02 — Blistering or delamination

Incompatible surface properties, uneven slurry distribution or insufficient bonding during curing can produce localized separation and reduce board integrity.

03

03 — Water damage

An unsuitable facer can allow moisture to reach the gypsum core. Compare liquid-water protection against the actual damp-environment or exterior-sheathing requirements.

04

04 — Wrinkles and uneven surfaces

Check coating uniformity, tensile properties and facer deformation during continuous production. Fire-resistant board applications also require the specified system fire-performance review.

04 — THREE FACER DIRECTIONS

Three facer directions

Use the gypsum formulation and finished-board requirements to compare material structures, rather than selecting by surface appearance alone.

01 — Controlled permeability

A balanced coating structure supports slurry penetration while maintaining surface coverage. Review coating density, fiberglass structure and permeability with the intended gypsum formulation to assess mechanical interlocking and facer-to-core bonding.

02 — High water resistance

Water-resistant coating can help reduce liquid-water penetration through the facer. Selected formulations can be reviewed for exterior sheathing that needs additional weathering and UV resistance. Confirm coating uniformity, moisture exposure and application compatibility.

03 — Dimensional stability

Consistent tensile properties and controlled coating uniformity support processing during continuous board manufacture. Review roll width, alignment, dimensional stability and line compatibility where wrinkles, deformation or handling waste are current problems.

05 — MATERIAL ROUTES TO COMPARE

Material routes to compare

Match each route to the required board construction and intended use.

01 — Controlled-permeability coated veil

For balanced gypsum slurry penetration and facer-to-core bonding.

02 — High water-resistance coated veil

For moisture-resistant gypsum boards needing greater protection against liquid-water penetration.

03 — UV-resistant coated veil

For selected exterior sheathing applications with sunlight and weathering requirements.

04 — Reinforced fiberglass composite facer

For additional mechanical strength, handling stability or specialized reinforcement.

06 — WHAT TO VERIFY ON YOUR PRODUCTION LINE

What to verify on your production line

Production engineers and technical buyers should compare the selected facer under actual board forming and drying conditions.

01 — Slurry and adhesion

Evaluate coating structure, permeability, slurry distribution and mechanical interlocking. Check adhesion compatibility and bonding performance with the intended formulation.

02 — Exposure and fire requirements

Assess liquid-water penetration and coating integrity for the intended application. Review applicable fire documentation for both the facer and the complete gypsum board system.

03 — Surface and roll consistency

Review tensile properties, coating uniformity and wrinkling resistance during forming and drying. Confirm roll dimensions, winding quality and material consistency for the continuous production line.

09 — QUESTIONS BEFORE A FACER TRIAL

Questions before a facer trial

Q1 — What controls slurry penetration?

Coating structure and permeability affect slurry interaction during board formation. Dense coatings may limit penetration; evaluate the required balance with the gypsum formulation and manufacturing process.

Q2 — Can the facer alone prevent delamination?

A compatible fiberglass structure and coating can support bonding, but final adhesion also depends on slurry formulation, forming conditions, drying and other production parameters.

Q3 — Are water and UV resistance the same requirement?

Selected formulations can improve surface resistance to liquid water and UV exposure. For exterior sheathing, review expected sunlight, weathering and installation conditions separately.

Q4 — What should accompany a sample request?

Provide board dimensions, type, process, slurry formulation and intended use. Describe poor penetration, delamination or water penetration so the trial has a specific question to resolve.

Complete board performance must still be verified through relevant testing.
