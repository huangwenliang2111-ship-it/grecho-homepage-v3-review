03 — CHECK THE FACER AGAINST THE BOARD DEFECT

Check the facer against the board defect

Even a core that meets insulation requirements can produce an unsuitable finished board if the facer and production conditions are mismatched.

01

01 — Moisture and surface deterioration

Consider exposure during storage, installation and service. Moisture passing through the facer or accumulating at the surface can increase deterioration under damp conditions.

02

02 — Weak bonding or separation

Review coating structure, porosity and adhesive distribution when boards show blistering or facer delamination. Check the actual core and lamination process.

03

03 — Wrinkles and dimensional change

Facer deformation during lamination, cutting or temperature changes can leave uneven surfaces. Compare dimensional stability with the board's handling requirements.

04

04 — Interrupted production and waste

Variations in roll width, coating uniformity, tensile properties or handling can disrupt continuous manufacture. Confirm requirements for the intended industrial or building application.

04 — THREE FACER DIRECTIONS

Three facer directions

Compare the direction with the intended insulation application and the actual production issue.

01 — Moisture-resistant coated veil

A moisture-resistant coating helps reduce liquid-water penetration through the facer. Review coating uniformity and exposure conditions with the intended roof or wall insulation system, including moisture encountered before and during installation.

02 — Bonding-compatible coated veil

Match veil structure, coating and porosity to the adhesive. Review surface characteristics, air permeability and lamination conditions where adhesive interaction or uneven facer-to-core bonding is the concern.

03 — Dimensionally stable coated veil

Controlled coating and consistent roll specifications support handling and cutting. Evaluate tensile strength, dimensional stability, roll width, winding quality and line compatibility where misalignment, wrinkles or processing waste occur.

05 — MATERIAL ROUTES TO COMPARE

Material routes to compare

Narrow the material review using the board specification and production requirements.

01 — Moisture-resistant coated veil

02 — High-bonding compatibility coated veil

04 — Reinforced fiberglass composite facer

For selected systems needing additional tensile strength and handling support.

06 — WHAT TO VERIFY BEFORE QUALIFICATION

What to verify before qualification

Assess the selected facer with the core and complete board construction under the relevant production conditions.

01 — Water exposure and porosity

Review liquid-water resistance for the intended application. Balance moisture protection with air permeability and coating consistency according to the manufacturing process.

02 — Bonding and dimensional behavior

Evaluate surface properties, coating structure and adhesive compatibility. Check resistance to deformation, wrinkling and dimensional changes during production and handling.

03 — Fire documentation and roll performance

Review applicable fire classification documents for both the selected facer and complete insulation board system. Confirm roll dimensions, winding quality, tensile properties and line compatibility.

09 — QUESTIONS BEFORE A FACER TRIAL

Questions before a facer trial

Q1 — Can a facer help limit liquid-water penetration?

Yes. A suitable moisture-resistant coating can help limit liquid water penetration through the facer. However, the overall moisture performance also depends on the mineral wool core, edges, joints, and the complete insulation system.

Q2 — How should bonding be validated?

By matching the coating structure, surface characteristics, and porosity to the adhesive and lamination process, manufacturers can improve bonding consistency. Adhesion should be validated using the actual board construction and processing conditions.

Q3 — Can material properties be customized for our line?

GRECHO can review requirements such as roll width, material weight, coating properties, air permeability, and tensile strength to identify suitable standard or customized material options, subject to technical and production feasibility.

Q4 — What should accompany a sample request?

Provide core type, board dimensions, adhesive, production method and intended application. Explain whether moisture, bonding, wrinkling or another production defect is the priority.
