02 — BALANCE SURFACE COVERAGE AND AIRFLOW

Balance surface coverage and airflow

The facing changes how sound interacts with the porous core, so select it as part of the panel design.

01 — Excessive airflow resistance

Dense or heavy coatings may restrict airflow and change how sound energy reaches the core.

02 — Uneven permeability

Coating distribution or structural variation can cause different airflow characteristics across the material.

03 — Coverage trade-offs

More coating for opacity can increase airflow resistance; compare appearance and acoustic requirements together.

04 — Core compatibility

Core material, density and thickness affect which facing characteristics suit the target response.

03 — CHOOSE PERMEABILITY FOR THE COMPLETE PANEL

Choose permeability for the complete panel

Engineered coating and veil structures provide different airflow capacities. Compare grades against the acoustic core and sound absorption target while checking surface uniformity and manufacturing compatibility.

01 — Controlled coating

Coating distribution helps maintain consistent surface appearance and the intended permeability.

02 — Stable fiberglass substrate

The lightweight structure supports handling, lamination and cutting during panel conversion.

04 — PERMEABILITY OPTIONS

Permeability options

The best facing is the one that suits the acoustic construction, rather than the one with the highest permeability.

01 — Balanced permeability

A controlled balance of decorative coverage and airflow for conventional mineral wool or glass wool ceiling panels needing a consistent surface appearance.

02 — High permeability

A more open coating structure offers lower airflow resistance than more heavily coated options. Evaluate it for airflow-sensitive ceiling or wall panel designs.

03 — Customized permeability

Select coating and facing characteristics for the desired balance of coverage, airflow resistance and acoustic response. Confirm available grades and customization ranges.

06 — FACING AND SOUND-ABSORBING CORE

Facing and sound-absorbing core

The facing supplies a decorative surface with selectable airflow paths. Mineral wool or glass wool forms the primary sound-absorbing structure beneath it.

01 — Permeable coated veil

An engineered coating and lightweight fiberglass substrate provide the finish and selected airflow characteristics.

02 — Porous acoustic core

The core's fiber network dissipates sound energy. Facing resistance, core thickness, density and panel construction all affect the finished response.

11 — AIRFLOW AND ACOUSTIC QUESTIONS

Airflow and acoustic questions

Q1 — Does higher permeability always improve absorption?

No. Higher air permeability does not automatically produce better sound absorption. The optimum facing characteristics depend on the acoustic core, thickness, density, frequency range, and complete panel construction.

Q2 — Does coating affect airflow?

Coating weight, distribution and structure influence resistance. Balance decorative coverage with the intended acoustic performance.

Q3 — Can the facing improve NRC?

A suitably selected facing may contribute to achieving the desired finished-panel sound absorption performance. However, NRC and other acoustic ratings must be determined through testing of the complete panel system.

Q4 — Can it be used on ceilings and walls?

Both can be evaluated. Final suitability depends on panel design, core material, lamination and the intended acoustic requirements.
