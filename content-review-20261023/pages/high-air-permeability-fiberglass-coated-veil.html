HIGH-PERMEABILITY ACOUSTIC FACING SOLUTIONS

High-Air-Permeability Fiberglass Coated Veil

Let Sound Pass Through. Designed for Acoustic Performance.

A permeable fiberglass facing for compatible mineral wool and glass wool acoustic panels. Select airflow characteristics alongside surface coverage and core properties, then evaluate the finished ceiling or wall panel.

  • Selectable airflow resistance
  • Consistent surface texture
  • Acoustic core compatibility

AIRFLOW MATTERS

Can the Facing Limit Your Acoustic Panel's Performance?

The facing changes how sound interacts with the porous core, so select it as part of the panel design.

01

Excessive airflow resistance

Dense or heavy coatings may restrict airflow and change how sound energy reaches the core.

02

Uneven permeability

Coating distribution or structural variation can cause different airflow characteristics across the material.

03

Coverage trade-offs

More coating for opacity can increase airflow resistance; compare appearance and acoustic requirements together.

04

Core compatibility

Core material, density and thickness affect which facing characteristics suit the target response.

Stable fiberglass substrate

CONTROL THE AIRFLOW

Different Permeability Levels. Different Acoustic Possibilities.

Select permeability for the complete acoustic construction; the highest permeability is not always appropriate.

01

Balanced permeability

Balanced decorative coverage and airflow for mineral wool or glass wool ceiling panels requiring a consistent surface.

02

High permeability

An open coating offers lower airflow resistance than heavier coatings. Evaluate it for airflow-sensitive ceiling or wall panels.

03

Customized permeability

Balance coverage, airflow resistance and acoustic response, then confirm available grades and customization ranges.

HOW IT WORKS

A Thin Facing. An Important Part of Sound Absorption.

The facing supplies a decorative surface with selectable airflow paths. Mineral wool or glass wool forms the primary sound-absorbing structure beneath it.

Permeable coated veil

An engineered coating and lightweight fiberglass substrate provide the finish and selected airflow characteristics.

Porous acoustic core

The core's fiber network dissipates sound energy. Facing resistance, core thickness, density and panel construction all affect the finished response.

FAQ

Questions Manufacturers Ask Before Choosing a High-Permeability Facer

Does higher permeability always improve absorption?

No. Higher air permeability does not automatically produce better sound absorption. The optimum facing characteristics depend on the acoustic core, thickness, density, frequency range, and complete panel construction.

Does coating affect airflow?

Coating weight, distribution and structure influence resistance. Balance decorative coverage with the intended acoustic performance.

Can the facing improve NRC?

A suitably selected facing may contribute to achieving the desired finished-panel sound absorption performance. However, NRC and other acoustic ratings must be determined through testing of the complete panel system.

Can it be used on ceilings and walls?

Both can be evaluated. Final suitability depends on panel design, core material, lamination and the intended acoustic requirements.
