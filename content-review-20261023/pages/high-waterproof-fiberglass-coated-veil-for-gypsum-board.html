High-Waterproof Fiberglass Coated Veil for Gypsum Board

A water-resistant coated fiberglass facing for suitable gypsum boards. Evaluate liquid-water penetration and slurry compatibility together, with grade-specific testing of the complete board or roofing assembly.

Key highlights:

  • Water-resistant coating
  • Stable fiberglass substrate

02 — WATER EXPOSURE AND BOARD INTEGRITY

Water exposure and board integrity

Excessive moisture reaching a susceptible gypsum core can affect cohesion, adhesion and dimensional consistency.

01 — Liquid-water penetration

An insufficiently resistant facing may allow water through the surface to the gypsum core.

02 — Core deterioration

Moisture can contribute to softening or loss of cohesion in moisture-sensitive core materials.

03 — Facing separation

Moisture exposure and unsuitable bonding can increase delamination risk.

04 — Dimensional changes

Changes within the board may cause deformation, surface irregularities or reduced consistency.

03 — BALANCE WATER RESISTANCE AND CORE BONDING

Balance water resistance and core bonding

A selected coating helps limit liquid water penetration through the facing. Controlled surface coverage and a stable fiberglass substrate support surface consistency, handling and board processing.

01 — Coating coverage

Review coating uniformity to reduce localized variation in protection across the facing surface.

02 — Production compatibility

Evaluate coating, permeability and substrate with gypsum slurry bonding, unwinding, forming and subsequent processing.

04 — COMPARE MOISTURE BEHAVIOR

Compare moisture behavior

Absorbency and resistance to liquid-water penetration serve different purposes. Choose the response required by the board design.

01 — Standard fiberglass facer

A stable facing direction that may offer limited liquid-water resistance without an appropriate protective coating.

02 — High-absorbency fiberglass facer

A structure intended for greater moisture uptake and selected permeability, distinct from a facing designed to restrict liquid-water entry.

03 — High-Waterproof coated veil

An engineered water-resistant coating helps reduce surface liquid-water penetration for suitable moisture-resistant gypsum board designs.

06 — WATER-RESISTANT FACING AND GYPSUM CORE

Water-resistant facing and gypsum core

The coated fiberglass substrate forms the water-resistant surface. The gypsum core provides the primary body and thickness of the board.

01 — Water-resistant surface coating

A selected formulation limits liquid-water penetration through the facing and provides consistent protective coverage.

02 — Compatible gypsum core

Evaluate coating distribution, facing permeability and slurry characteristics together to achieve suitable interfacial adhesion without compromising the intended water resistance.

07 — SELECT FOR THE INTENDED EXPOSURE

Select for the intended exposure

Coating grade and finished-board evidence determine which application to evaluate.

01 — Moisture-resistant interior boards

For selected humid indoor environments or occasional moisture exposure, using an appropriate facing and board system.

02 — Higher surface water resistance

Evaluate the High-Waterproof grade for boards and specialized sheathing needing reduced liquid-water penetration through the facing.

03 — Exterior sheathing

Weather-resistant options require evaluation against the environmental exposure and complete-system testing.

Validate the water resistance and bond

Test under exposure conditions relevant to the finished board and with the intended slurry and drying process.

01 — Define requirements

Share application, core formulation, water-resistance target, dimensions and line conditions.

02 — Compare samples

03 — Run production trials

04 — Confirm the grade

Agree grade, roll dimensions, coating and performance requirements using test results.

11 — WATER-RESISTANCE QUESTIONS

Water-resistance questions

Q1 — Does the facing protect the entire core?

A water-resistant coating can reduce surface penetration. Overall protection also depends on board construction, edges, joints, core formulation and installation.

Q2 — Can the coating affect slurry bonding?

Yes. Coating structure, permeability and slurry characteristics affect interfacial bonding. Evaluate the grade through production trials and bonding tests.

Q3 — Can it replace a roofing waterproof membrane?

No. A water-resistant fiberglass facing is not a substitute for the waterproof membrane required in a roofing system. Its role is to support the performance of the gypsum cover board within an appropriately designed roofing assembly.

Q4 — What can be customized?

Coating, basis weight, roll width and other properties may be available, depending on the selected grade and manufacturing feasibility.
