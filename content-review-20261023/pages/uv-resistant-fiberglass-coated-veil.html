02 — APPLICATION CHALLENGES

Eyebrow: WHEN SUNLIGHT BECOMES A CHALLENGE

What Happens When UV Exposure Attacks the Surface?

Sunlight can affect more than a panel's appearance.

Exterior building panels may experience prolonged sunlight exposure during transportation, storage, installation, or service.

Without suitable UV resistance, certain facing coatings and polymer-based components can undergo surface degradation, potentially affecting appearance, coating integrity, and material performance.

Four common concerns highlight the importance of choosing an appropriate UV-resistant facing.

01 — Surface Fading

Prolonged sunlight exposure may cause discoloration or fading in UV-sensitive surface coatings, resulting in an inconsistent panel appearance.

02 — Coating Degradation

UV radiation can contribute to coating aging, chalking, or embrittlement in susceptible materials, reducing surface integrity.

03 — Surface Cracking

Repeated UV and temperature exposure may accelerate deterioration in unsuitable coatings, potentially resulting in microcracks or localized surface defects.

04 — Reduced Surface Protection

As an exposed facing deteriorates, its ability to provide consistent protective coverage may be affected, increasing the vulnerability of underlying materials.

Small Surface Changes. Bigger Performance Concerns.

Selecting a UV-resistant facing helps manufacturers address sunlight-related surface aging before it becomes a challenge for finished-panel quality.

11 — FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Eyebrow: FAQ

Questions Manufacturers Ask Before Choosing a UV-Resistant Facer

Q1 — What is UV-Resistant Fiberglass Coated Veil?

It is a fiberglass veil substrate combined with an engineered UV-resistant coating, designed to help reduce ultraviolet-related surface aging in compatible building panel systems.

Q2 — How is it different from standard fiberglass coated veil?

Standard fiberglass coated veil provides surface coverage and a stable fiberglass structure.

UV-resistant grades incorporate coating characteristics specifically intended to improve durability under UV exposure.

Q3 — Is it suitable for exterior gypsum sheathing?

UV-resistant fiberglass facing can be evaluated for exterior gypsum sheathing systems requiring enhanced surface stability during outdoor exposure.

Final suitability depends on gypsum core bonding, moisture resistance, finished-board testing, and installation requirements.

Q4 — Can it be used with PIR/PUR insulation boards?

Yes. UV-resistant fiberglass facing options can be evaluated for compatibility with PIR/PUR insulation board manufacturing.

The selected coating and substrate must be compatible with the foam formulation and lamination process.

Q5 — Does 6,000 hours of QUV testing mean the facer will last 6,000 hours outdoors?

No. QUV testing is an accelerated laboratory aging method.

The relationship between laboratory exposure and outdoor service life depends on UV intensity, temperature, humidity, geographic location, and other conditions.

Q6 — Can the facing remain exposed outdoors for six months?

GRECHO publishes an up-to-six-month outdoor UV protection claim for selected insulation-facing products.

The permitted exposure duration for a specific gypsum board or PIR/PUR panel should be verified using applicable test data, construction conditions, and the manufacturer's installation requirements.

Q7 — Can UV resistance and water resistance be combined?

Selected facing formulations can combine UV-resistant and water-resistant surface characteristics.

The appropriate product grade should be chosen according to the intended exposure conditions and finished-panel requirements.

Q8 — Can I request samples for production testing?

Yes. Share your panel structure, manufacturing process, and UV resistance requirements with GRECHO to discuss suitable samples and technical evaluation.
