02 — WHAT DEFINES A QUALITY CEILING SURFACE?

Eyebrow: SURFACE QUALITY MATTERS

What Makes a Consistent Acoustic Ceiling Surface?

The quality of a ceiling panel is often judged by what people see first.

Even small variations in the facing material can become noticeable across a finished ceiling installation. From surface whiteness to texture and light reflection, every detail contributes to the overall appearance.

For manufacturers, achieving a consistent ceiling surface involves four essential considerations.

01 — Color Consistency

Uneven whiteness between panels can create visible differences across large ceiling installations.

02 — Surface Uniformity

Variations in fiber distribution or coating texture may cause irregular surface patterns and an inconsistent appearance.

03 — Light Reflection

Differences in surface texture and coating uniformity can create unwanted variations in light reflection under the same lighting conditions.

04 — Acoustic Compatibility

A facing material with unsuitable air permeability may restrict airflow into the acoustic core and influence the sound absorption performance of the finished panel.

Small Surface Differences. Noticeable Results.

Choosing the right facing material helps manufacturers manage these factors before they affect finished-panel quality.

06 — PRODUCT STRUCTURE

Eyebrow: HOW IT WORKS

A Thin Facing. An Essential Surface Layer.

The facing material connects visual appearance with acoustic function.

White Acoustic Ceiling Facer is applied to the visible surface of acoustic panels, typically over a mineral wool or glass wool core.

Its lightweight structure helps provide a refined surface finish without replacing the sound-absorbing function of the core.

Layer 01 —Fiberglass Coated Veil

Combining a lightweight, dimensionally stable fiberglass veil with a uniform white surface coating, GRECHO Fiberglass Coated Veil delivers a refined white finish, consistent surface coverage, and a fine, uniform texture while supporting reliable processing in acoustic ceiling panel production.

Layer 02 — Acoustic Core

Mineral wool or glass wool forms the primary sound-absorbing structure of the ceiling panel.

Designed to Work Together.

The facing material and acoustic core must be evaluated as a complete panel system to achieve the desired balance between appearance and sound absorption.

11 — FAQ

Eyebrow: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Questions Manufacturers Ask Before Choosing a Facer

Q1 — What is White Acoustic Ceiling Facer made of?

It consists of a fiberglass veil substrate with a white surface coating, designed for use as the visible facing layer of acoustic ceiling panels.

Q2 — Can the facing material affect sound absorption?

Yes. Facing airflow resistance can influence the acoustic performance of the complete panel. The appropriate facing should be selected and tested together with the acoustic core.

Q3 — Is it suitable for both mineral wool and glass wool?

GRECHO offers white fiberglass facing materials suitable for evaluation with both mineral wool and glass wool acoustic panels. The final selection depends on the panel design and production process.

Q4 — Can I choose different levels of white surface coverage?

Yes. Standard white and high-hiding white facing options can be considered according to the underlying core color and desired surface appearance.

Q5 — Can the roll width and material specifications be customized?

Customization options may include roll width, basis weight, coating characteristics, and surface properties, subject to product grade and manufacturing feasibility.

Q6 — Can I request samples for production testing?

Yes. You can share your material requirements with GRECHO to discuss suitable samples and arrange evaluation before larger-scale production.
