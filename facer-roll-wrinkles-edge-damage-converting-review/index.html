Facer-Roll Wrinkles and Edge Damage: A Converting Review Before Lamination

Locate facer-roll wrinkles and edge damage before lamination. Compare retained samples, process stages and observations without guessing the root cause.

When a glass-fibre facer wrinkles or develops edge damage during converting, first establish where the defect begins. Compare retained material before and after the relevant operation, keep roll identity and running direction visible, and distinguish an incoming defect from one introduced during unwinding, guiding, slitting or lamination. A defect map is more useful than an immediate change of material grade.

The practical buyer question is: what evidence should the converter and material supplier review before deciding that a facer is unsuitable for the board line?

This article starts after the incoming facer-roll inspection. It proposes a way to investigate converting observations; it does not prescribe machine settings or replace the equipment supplier’s operating instructions.

Find the first changed condition

A photograph of a finished board cannot, by itself, establish when a crease formed. Build a short record of the material’s condition at successive stages: incoming retained sample, unwind, the relevant converting operation, and the laminated panel.

Use observations already available through safe inspection points or approved camera systems. Collect samples only through the site’s safe procedures. Physical inspection, cleaning and adjustments belong to qualified operators working under the machine’s isolation and guarding requirements—not to someone reaching into a moving web.

Record the side being viewed, machine direction, operator-side edge and approximate position along the roll. Photograph each retained sample with the same orientation and a scale. A close-up identifies the defect; a wider image locates it across the web.

When a wrinkle is first seen after bonding, keep the assembly history

First seen after bonding does not mean first formed there. Keep the last available observation of the unbonded facer beside the immediate post-bonding view and any later view after conditioning. If the upstream view is missing, mark the onset as unresolved.

Retain the adhesive identity and revision, application pattern and quantity basis where recorded, the substrate or core identity and surface condition, contact and pressing history, and inspection timing. These are comparison inputs, not a diagnosis of adhesive, facer or substrate fault. Relevant checks and adjustments remain with qualified personnel under the line’s safety procedures.

Turn symptoms into questions, not verdicts

General web-handling guidance identifies alignment, roller condition, incoming-web quality, unsupported spans and tension control as possible contributors to wrinkling. Both excessive and insufficient tension can be involved. These are investigation categories, not a diagnosis of a particular GRECHO facer. [1]

Use the following review map to decide what evidence to collect next.

First reliable observation Question to investigate Useful comparison
A crease is already present in the retained incoming material Was the feature present before the converting trial? Packaging/roll photos and an unprocessed retained sample
The incoming sample is flat, but a wrinkle appears downstream At which identified operation does the condition change? Images immediately before and after that operation
Edge damage appears after slitting Is the same edge condition visible before cutting? Labelled uncut material and retained cut strips
A mark is seen only on the laminated board Is it also present on the unlaminated facer or substrate? The three specimens viewed under consistent lighting
The symptom appears only during a transition Which logged process condition changed at that time? Start-up, stable-running and transition records

Do not erase uncertainty by writing “bad roll” or “machine fault” too early. A useful first conclusion is narrower: “The retained incoming sample does not show the crease; the earliest recorded crease is after the identified guide section.” That statement helps the next investigation without assigning an unsupported cause.

Separate edge damage from usable-width decisions

An edge photograph needs a reference. Is it the original supplied edge, a newly slit edge, or a trimmed edge on the finished board? Those are different inspection points.

Mark the intended usable width separately from sacrificial trim. Then ask whether the observed damage remains inside the retained panel area, whether it continues along the roll, and whether loose material is being carried into the bonding area. Do not decide acceptance from the word “fraying” alone.

For a repeat trial, propose a simple retained-sample set: one incoming specimen, one after the operation under investigation, and one finished-panel specimen. Keep the identification format consistent with the lot and roll traceability record.

This is a proposed review record, not a universal sampling frequency. The quantity of retained material and the release criteria must suit the agreed trial and the customer’s quality process.

Compare changes against a reference condition

Changing material, line speed, tension, adhesive and substrate together may produce a better panel, but it leaves the reason unresolved. Where a qualified process team approves a follow-up trial, retain a reference condition and document each intentional change.

A compact comparison record should include material construction and roll ID, width, running direction, the process condition under investigation, the first observed defect location, and the condition of the finished panel. Where settings are recorded, copy the actual logged value and unit; do not estimate them from memory.

Illustrative case—not a GRECHO test result: a retained incoming strip is undamaged, while two strips collected after the same cutting operation show a repeated edge feature. That supports a focused review of the cutting stage. It does not prove that every roll is defective, nor does it establish which component or setting caused the feature.

Plan material and process comparisons without mixing their conclusions

Before another safe, technically authorized comparison, name the material or roll question and the process question separately. The matrix below is a planning aid, not a required four-run sequence or a set of machine settings. Record actual conditions, deviations, retained specimens and the first observed change for each condition used.

Documented condition Reference facer or roll Candidate facer or roll
Reference process condition Confirm the current reference observation. Compare the candidate without an intentional process change.
One defined, authorized process change Observe the reference under that same change, if safe and relevant. Observe whether the candidate responds differently.

A symptom repeatedly associated with one roll supports a roll-linked hypothesis, but does not prove supplied-material fault or the state of its lot. A symptom that changes with the same process condition for both materials supports process investigation. A different response by candidate and reference raises a material–process interaction question. When roll, threading and setup change together, the result is confounded: retain the observation without assigning it to one cause. For broader trial design, see the controlled board-trial comparison guide.

Worked example: the roll and setup change together

Hypothetical teaching example, not a GRECHO result: a candidate roll shows a right-side wrinkle. The team changes to a reference roll, rethreads the web and checks the guide setup; the next panel looks flat. Material identity and setup both changed. The supported statement is that the wrinkle was not observed under the new combined condition. Keep both identified samples, the before/after observations and the changeover log before deciding whether a controlled follow-up can isolate either explanation.

Agree what “ready for the next trial” means

The most useful outcome is a bounded decision: the material can proceed under a defined reference condition, an affected section requires further review, or evidence is still insufficient to release the trial.

Agree the decision before expanding the trial. Define which visible defects are unacceptable within the usable panel area, who reviews retained samples, and which evidence is required to lift a hold. A visually improved web does not automatically prove the finished board’s adhesion, durability or other performance.

For materials intended for insulation boards, keep this converting review connected to the relevant PIR/PUR facer application rather than treating web handling as a complete board qualification.

Send a four-part review pack and request a bounded decision

Link the review request to material identity (construction or revision, lot, roll, side and position), the defect timeline (oriented before/after specimens and inspection times), relevant process history (actual available readings, operating phase, substrate and adhesive identity where relevant), and the decision required (material review, equipment check, controlled comparison or hold). Mark missing evidence as missing rather than filling gaps from memory.

Close with the sequence observation → hypothesis → comparison → supported action. A flatter web addresses only the appearance condition evaluated. It does not by itself establish bond durability, finished-board performance, whole-lot conformity or supplier responsibility.

Frequently asked questions

Does a wrinkle mean the facer grade is unsuitable?

Not necessarily. First establish whether it was present before processing and where it first becomes visible. Material suitability and process compatibility should be reviewed together using identified specimens.

Can increasing tension solve the problem?

There is no universal adjustment. Web-handling guidance recognises problems associated with both too much and too little tension. Any adjustment requires the qualified process team and the equipment supplier’s limits. [1]

What should be included in a supplier review request?

Provide the facer construction or reference, roll ID, application, running direction, defect location, and labelled before/after photographs. Share the relevant process record through an agreed channel rather than sending an isolated finished-board image.
