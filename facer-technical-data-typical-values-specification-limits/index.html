Typical Value, Specification Limit or Test Result? How to Read Facer Technical Data

A buyer-focused guide to distinguishing descriptive material data, specification limits, test methods and sample-specific results. Includes a document-label comparison and a clearly hypothetical numerical example without prescribing GRECHO tolerances or purchasing limits.

A typical value describes an indicative property, a specification limit defines a requirement within its stated scope, a test method describes how a property is evaluated, and a sample result records the outcome for the tested material. These statements serve different purposes. Before using a number to compare or accept fiberglass facers, identify its label, property, unit, method, specimen and decision scope.

A data sheet can help narrow a material discussion without defining every acceptance condition for a delivery. Equally, a test report can answer a precise question without establishing that every future roll will have the reported result. The practical task is to understand what each statement supports before copying it into a comparison sheet or purchasing requirement.

1. Identify the type of statement before comparing the number

Read the heading, footnotes and referenced specification around a value. “Typical”, “nominal”, “minimum” and “measured” are not interchangeable labels. The interpretations below are review prompts: confirm the meaning used in the particular document rather than assuming that all suppliers use identical definitions.

How to interpret labels in facer technical documentation
Document label What it can mean Required context Unsafe inference Buyer follow-up
Typical value An indicative or representative property value Supplier’s definition, data basis and test conditions Every roll is guaranteed to meet this exact value What information does “typical” summarise here?
Nominal value A named target or product designation used in that document Whether it is a designation, target or specified requirement A tolerance exists because a nominal number is shown Where are any applicable limits defined?
Specification limit A stated minimum, maximum or boundary of a permitted range Applicable specification, version, method and acceptance basis The limit applies to every construction, condition or property Which product and decision does this limit govern?
Test method The approach or referenced procedure used to evaluate a property Method/version and relevant specimen and test conditions A standard number is a performance rating or certificate What was measured under this method?
Sample-specific result A reported outcome for an identified tested item or sample set Sample identity, sampling basis, condition and reporting basis The result proves all lots or future deliveries What material population, if any, does the sample represent?
Minimum observed result The lowest value within the reported observations Number of observations and how specimens were selected This is a guaranteed minimum for supply Is this an observed statistic or a specified requirement?

This distinction is visible in industrial documentation. For example, 3M’s electronics-bonding guidance distinguishes typical TDS information from the specifications, methods and results in its product certificates of analysis. That is an example of one supplier’s document system—not a universal rule for document titles or a statement about GRECHO’s available documents. [1]

2. Keep the property, unit and method together

Create the comparison row around the measured property, not the number alone. Preserve the stated unit and the conditions needed to interpret it. For a facer, also identify whether the figure concerns the complete coated construction, a component layer or a prepared assembly.

Check the details that matter to the property. For tensile data, retain the direction and specimen/test conditions. For basis weight, identify the material or layer being weighed. For air permeability, retain the pressure difference, test basis and material condition. Do not remove those details simply to make a spreadsheet narrower.

A method name is not a result. JCGM’s International Vocabulary of Metrology distinguishes a measurement method from the more detailed measurement procedure used to carry it out. For a practical supplier comparison, request the relevant procedure details when the method reference alone leaves the test basis unclear. [2]

Property-specific questions belong in the relevant technical review: see facer tensile data and MD/CD conditions, basis weight versus thickness and air-permeability methods, pressure and units.

3. Keep typical and nominal values separate from acceptance limits

A typical value may help describe a material direction, but the label alone does not define a pass/fail boundary. Do not turn it into a guaranteed minimum, maximum or tolerance without a separately defined requirement.

Likewise, a nominal value should not be treated as an average test result unless the document says that is what it represents. A product designation, a manufacturing target and the mean of a sample set answer different questions.

For acceptance, identify the applicable requirement and how conformity is determined. Where a laboratory provides a statement of conformity, clarify the decision rule and the treatment of measurement uncertainty where relevant. ILAC G8 addresses decision rules and conformity statements; it is not a table of universal acceptance limits for facers. [3]

Hypothetical example: a result below the typical value

The following values are invented solely to explain document interpretation. They are not GRECHO specifications, recommended tolerances or actual test results.

Hypothetical basis-weight example — not GRECHO data or acceptance recommendations
Record Hypothetical statement Meaning in this example
Technical data sheet Typical basis weight: 200 g/m² Descriptive information; no acceptance limit is defined by this line
Agreed material specification Minimum basis weight: 190 g/m² under the specified test and acceptance conditions A defined lower requirement for the stated material and scope
Sample report Reported result: 194 g/m² for the identified sample An observation to assess against the applicable requirement

The reported result is below the typical value, but that alone is not a failure. It is numerically above the hypothetical minimum; a valid acceptance decision still depends on the applicable method, specimen, sampling and decision rule. None of these three lines, by itself, establishes the conformity of an entire delivery or any other performance property.

4. Check what was sampled and what the reported figure summarises

Read the sample reference before interpreting the result. Establish whether the report concerns a submitted coupon, specimens from an identified roll, or a sample set selected under a stated plan. Then check the material revision, relevant lot/roll references, preparation and conditioning.

Ask what the number summarises. It might be an individual reading, an average, a minimum observed value or another reported statistic. Without that distinction, a supplier’s average can accidentally be compared with another supplier’s lowest observation.

The JCGM vocabulary treats a measurement result as more than an isolated number: it includes values attributed to the quantity being measured and relevant accompanying information. That supports a practical purchasing habit—keep the context attached to the result. [4]

When sampling information is absent, record the limitation. Do not silently describe the value as “batch representative” or “production verified”. Ask what conclusion the sampling basis was intended to support.

5. Decide whether the two data sets answer the same question

Before ranking two materials, check whether the comparison is between the same kinds of statements. Two typical values can support a preliminary discussion when their basis is sufficiently understood. A typical value and a guaranteed limit should not be treated as equivalent evidence merely because their units match.

Next, compare the tested constructions and conditions. A complete facer and its uncoated base layer are not the same test object. A result from one identified state should not be relabelled as a result for another state without supporting evidence.

Finally, return to the intended decision. A data comparison may help select the next candidate for sampling; it need not establish suitability for every board construction. When essential information is missing, use “clarification required” rather than assigning an unsupported winner.

For example, a higher typical tensile figure does not automatically outrank a lower specified minimum. First establish whether the direction, specimen, method and statistical basis are comparable—and whether tensile performance is the requirement actually limiting the application.

6. Turn the review into a precise supplier question

A focused request is more useful than asking whether a data sheet is “accurate”. Identify the product direction, the disputed line and the decision you need to make.

We are reviewing this facer for an identified board application. Your document lists the property as a typical value. Please confirm the document revision, the material and specimen covered, the test method and conditions, and whether the number is an individual result or a summary. Please also clarify any applicable specification limits and the acceptance basis. We need this information to decide the next sample-evaluation step.

Keep the reply with the original document and its revision. In the comparison sheet, separate “data understood”, “requirement agreed” and “material accepted”. A useful clarification can close the first question without closing the other two.

Use the GRECHO Resource Center to identify the relevant document category, then scope the technical discussion to the material and board application. Document selection and data interpretation are related tasks, but they are not the same decision.

Frequently asked questions

Is a typical value a guaranteed specification?

Not on the strength of the label alone. Confirm how the document defines the value and whether a separate applicable specification establishes limits. Do not convert descriptive data into a purchasing guarantee by copying it into a new table.

Can a sample fail because its result is lower than the typical value?

Not merely for that reason. Compare the result with the applicable requirement using the relevant method and acceptance basis. “Below typical” and “outside specification” are different statements. The hypothetical example above illustrates the distinction, not a recommended tolerance.

Does a recognised test method prove that the material is suitable for our board?

No. A method identifies how a property is evaluated; suitability also depends on the tested material, construction, result and intended requirement. Keep material-level evidence and the board application’s evaluation requirements distinct.

Discuss the technical data needed for your application

Share your company, country, board application, selected facer direction and the property you need to clarify. Identify whether the next decision concerns material screening, sample comparison or an agreed specification review.

GRECHO supports application-matched facer discussions and coordination of relevant technical documentation. Applicable document scope and availability are confirmed for the selected material direction. Discuss your facer technical-data requirements with GRECHO.
