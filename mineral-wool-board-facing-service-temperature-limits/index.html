Mineral-Wool Board Facings: Insulation and Facing Temperature Limits

A mineral-wool product's service rating does not set the temperature limit for every facing. Match each statement to the identified construction, location and exposure.

An insulation product’s maximum service-temperature rating is not automatically the allowable temperature of its facing. Before selecting a facing for a mineral-wool board, identify the rated product, the exact facing, the bonding interface and the location to which each temperature statement applies.

This also works in the other direction: a facing limit is not automatically a limit on the temperature of every part of the assembly. The review needs the proposed construction and its relevant exposure information, not a single number copied across all layers.

The examples below are named ROCKWOOL stone-wool products. They illustrate how manufacturers separate temperature information; they do not establish a temperature rating for GRECHO facers or approve a GRECHO construction for industrial or marine use.

1. Keep the insulation-product field and facing field separate

ROCKWOOL Technical Insulation’s ProRox SL 960 page lists a maximum service temperature in its product-properties table. A separate facing-information section identifies an optional reinforced aluminum-foil facing and gives a different recommended maximum temperature for facings. [1]

The point is not to borrow either value. The manufacturer has presented two distinct fields. A reviewer should retain their original descriptions and confirm the construction and conditions covered by each.

Do not relabel the product-properties field as a bare-core test unless the supporting document actually identifies that tested object. “Insulation-product rating,” “core result” and “facing limit” should not become interchangeable labels simply because they appear in one discussion.

In a proposed board specification, leave an unresolved facing field explicitly unresolved. Copying the insulation value into that blank would hide the very question the material review needs to answer.

2. Identify the facing combination, not just its visible material

The SeaRox FB 6050 page provides another manufacturer example. It lists reinforced aluminum-foil and specified glass-cloth facing options, then states a recommended temperature limit for facings separately from its application description and other product properties. [2]

Those options belong to that supplier’s product range. They do not make a limit universal for aluminum foil, glass cloth, coated fiberglass mat or any visually similar facing. Nor does a marine product example establish that a proposed GRECHO material is approved for the same service.

For the actual comparison, identify the facing reference and revision, its documented layers or coatings, the side in contact with the board, and the bonding-system information that can be shared. An equivalent-looking surface is not enough to establish an equivalent temperature basis.

This is a request for combination-specific evidence, not a diagnosis of which component would fail first. The public examples do not establish a failure mechanism for an untested alternative facing.

3. Put the temperature statement at a named location

Use a simple construction sketch to name the heat-exposed side, the facing position, the board and the interface being discussed. Then attach each available temperature statement to the location or object its source actually describes.

Where the documentation does not say which location a limit addresses, ask for clarification. Do not assume that a product rating is a measured outer-surface temperature, or that an exterior-facing limit describes the temperature at a remote heat source.

Likewise, a temperature capability is not a temperature prediction. The capability of an insulation product does not calculate the temperature that a proposed facing or bond line will experience. Record the available location-specific evidence and leave the assessment of an unknown temperature to the responsible application team.

Include the intended exposure description, such as continuous service or a stated cycle, when asking that question. This is information to clarify with the technical team, not an instruction to run a particular test or choose an operating setting.

Illustrative review—not a GRECHO installation: a development brief contains an insulation-product rating, but the drawing does not identify which side receives the proposed facing. Neither approval nor rejection of that facing follows from the number alone. First establish the position, the relevant exposure and the facing-specific documentation.

4. Use a location-based temperature review

The following table is an editorial document-review aid. It identifies useful questions before a facing decision; it does not provide thermal-design calculations or qualification criteria.

Information needed before transferring a temperature statement to a faced mineral-wool board
Information available Object or location to identify Next evidence question What remains unproven
Only an insulation-product service-temperature rating Exact product, document version and rated construction Is there separate facing information for the proposed combination? That every facing accepts the product’s stated temperature
A separate facing recommendation Facing reference, documented layers and the location addressed Does it cover the proposed facing and intended exposure? That the same limit applies to every part of the assembly
A known heat-source condition but an unclear facing position Heat-exposed side, facing side and relevant interface What location-specific temperature information supports the review? The facing temperature inferred from the insulation rating
A replacement with similar color or area weight The actual facing and bonding-system identities Which combination-specific temperature documents remain applicable? Temperature suitability based on appearance alone
A reaction-to-fire or noncombustibility document The classified product or construction and the question evaluated Where is the separate service-temperature information? A facing temperature limit or long-term bond performance
Only an initial room-temperature bond observation Prepared board/facing interface and observation conditions What interface evidence is needed for the intended exposure? Bond retention, service life or unrestricted use at temperature

A useful outcome is a defined information gap: for example, the insulation-product statement is available, while the proposed facing’s identity or temperature location still needs confirmation. That is more actionable than an unexplained “heat resistant” label.

5. Keep fire classification and interface performance as separate questions

A fire-related document should retain its own tested object and classification scope. It does not supply a missing service-temperature statement for another facing or a bond-performance result for the proposed interface. The SeaRox page’s separate facing information and fire-related properties illustrate why those document fields should remain distinct. [2]

Use the existing guide to Class A, A2-s1,d0 and noncombustibility evidence for fire terminology. This temperature review does not add a new fire classification or interpret a marine approval for a different material.

For the interface, label what has actually been observed. A room-temperature sample observation remains an observation under those conditions; it is not evidence of performance under every later exposure. The board/application team should define the unresolved interface question without treating the manufacturer’s facing recommendation as a diagnosis of adhesive degradation.

The broader mineral-wool facer sample-review guide addresses preparation and qualification planning. Here the additional task is attaching temperature evidence to the correct product, facing and location.

6. Send a construction question, not a request for one maximum number

For a useful first discussion, provide the board or slab reference, current and proposed facing references, a shareable layer sketch, facing position and bonding-system information. Include the original temperature field labels, document versions and the location or conditions they state.

Describe the intended exposure and which part of it is known. Keep an estimated or unverified location temperature clearly labelled rather than presenting it as a measurement. Explain the decision still needed: a facing-document match, an interface-evidence request or clarification from the board manufacturer.

GRECHO can support application-matched material discussions, sample review and coordination of available technical documents. A material direction and its document availability remain subject to application review; this does not promise a qualified high-temperature facing or industrial/marine supply approval.

Frequently asked questions

Which temperature limit belongs to the facing?

Use the statement that identifies the actual facing combination and its applicable location and conditions. An insulation-product value does not fill a missing facing-specific field.

Does the insulation rating predict the outer-surface temperature?

No. A rating describes a stated capability or evaluation; it does not calculate the surface temperature in the proposed assembly. Ask for the relevant location-specific assessment.

Can a noncombustibility statement replace service-temperature evidence?

No. Keep fire classification and service-temperature information attached to their own evaluated questions. Neither also supplies a missing interface-performance result.

Can a different fiberglass facing use the same limit?

Not without an applicable basis. Match the actual facing, coatings or other documented layers, bonding arrangement and exposure. The named supplier examples do not set a universal limit for fiberglass materials.

Does a facing limit automatically cap the heat-source temperature?

Not automatically. First identify where the facing sits and which location the limit addresses. Do not approve or reject an assembly by comparing numbers that describe different locations.

Sources and scope

[1] ROCKWOOL Technical Insulation: ProRox SL 960, facing information and product-properties sections, read October 1, 2026.

[2] ROCKWOOL Technical Insulation: SeaRox FB 6050, application, facing-information and product-properties sections, read October 1, 2026.

These are two named examples from one manufacturer, not independent cross-manufacturer validation. Their numerical limits are not reproduced as GRECHO values or operating recommendations. The table and illustrative scenario are editorial review aids; no temperature calculation, exposure procedure, interface-life claim or application approval is established here.
