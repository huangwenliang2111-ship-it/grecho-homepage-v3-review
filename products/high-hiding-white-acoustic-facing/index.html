Customer Problem

When a white facer still shows
what’s underneath.

For acoustic ceiling and wall panels, “white” is not enough. Core color, fiber shadows and uneven coating can still remain visible on the finished board.

01

Core show-through

Mineral wool or glass wool color remains visible through the facer.

02

Shade variation

Different areas of the same panel can appear uneven after lamination.

03

Double-layer complexity

Extra facing layers may be added just to improve coverage, increasing process steps.

04

Opacity vs. airflow

Simply adding more coating can improve hiding but may reduce air permeability.

Customer Pain

Visible core shadows make finished acoustic boards look inconsistent under real lighting.

What the board needs

Higher surface hiding with a controlled balance between appearance, airflow and processing stability.

GRECHO Solution

High hiding without treating opacity as the only goal.

The facing is developed as a system: fiberglass substrate, coating distribution, whiteness, surface consistency and airflow are considered together.

In Your Panel

How the facing works in an
acoustic board.

A thin facing. A major visual difference.

The facer remains a paper-like surface layer rather than a structural board component. Its role is to influence the appearance, air permeability, durability and processing behavior of the finished acoustic panel.
