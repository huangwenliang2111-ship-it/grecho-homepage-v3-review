What to Check Before a Wide-Width Facer Trial on a Continuous Board Line

A wider facer trial should answer a defined question. Use this pre-trial framework to align material identity, usable width, line constraints and the evidence needed for the next decision.

A first wider-roll trial can produce a board that looks acceptable while leaving the main question unanswered. Did the usable facer zone cover the board throughout the agreed process conditions? Was the result representative across the width? Was the same material actually compared, or did the contact side, coating or bonding process change at the same time?

Before booking the trial, agree seven things: the decision the trial should support, material identity, the usable-width drawing, roll and equipment limits, the reference construction, the sampling plan and the acceptance record. A sheet sample or small-roll result can support preparation; it does not release a wider roll for a production line.

This review framework is for board manufacturers, procurement and technical teams. It is not a record of a completed GRECHO customer trial or a machine operating procedure. Line operation, safe handling and numerical limits remain with the manufacturer’s authorised personnel and applicable equipment procedures.

1. Decide what the trial is allowed to prove

“Test the wider facer” is not a sufficiently clear objective. A trial could assess coverage, behaviour through the line, surface appearance, bonding or the ability to repeat an accepted construction. Those questions need different observations. Write one primary decision first, then identify the supporting checks.

For example: “Determine whether the agreed usable zone covers this board format without an unreviewed change in the material contact interface.” This is a trial question, not a promise that the answer will be positive. It also does not establish full commercial supply, a fire classification or the performance of every board made with that facer.

Identify who can accept the result. Procurement can confirm the offered material identity and supply scope, but the board manufacturer’s process and quality owners should define line suitability and finished-board acceptance. A smooth run alone does not replace those decisions.

2. Put the material and the width on the same revision

Start with the selected material construction and the roll label. Record the coating or surface version, any reinforcement, the intended contact face, machine direction and lot/roll identity. “The same grade” is not enough when the supplied revision or contact face cannot be traced.

Then attach a cross-width drawing. It should distinguish the delivered width from the continuous usable zone and from the area the board must actually retain. Show left and right edges, coating boundaries, coverage allowances and the point in the process at which the dimensions apply.

Use the existing usable-width and board-coverage guide for the dimensional calculation.

Do not add the same edge deduction twice. If a minimum usable coated width already excludes an agreed edge zone, a second deduction would distort the comparison. Equally, a nominal gross width should not be treated as fully usable without agreement.

Record before the trial What must be made explicit Suggested owner
Material revision Construction, coating, reinforcement and contact face Material supplier + technical team
Roll identity Lot, roll identifier and link to retained samples Supplier + receiving quality
Width drawing Usable zone, coverage zone, reference edge and measurement stage Board process team + supplier
Roll format Core, outside diameter, mass, length and winding orientation Supplier + line owner
Reference construction Board/core, bonding route and comparison conditions Process + quality
Acceptance record Observation locations, methods, timing and decision owner Quality + technical team

This table is a suggested handover record, not a new GRECHO product specification or an industry-wide minimum requirement.

3. Confirm that the proposed roll is a permitted trial input

An acceptable width calculation does not establish that a roll can be loaded, unwound and converted safely. Ask the equipment owner to confirm the proposed core, roll diameter, mass, winding direction and handling route against the actual line and its approved limits. Treat any unresolved limit as a pre-trial hold, not something to discover by trying the roll.

Use the established receiving process for packaging condition, edge damage, roll shape, label identity and any agreed splice declaration. Record the received condition before the roll enters the process so that a later surface or edge observation is not automatically attributed to the material formulation.

The incoming-roll inspection guide covers receiving checks in more detail.

Use the wrinkles and edge-damage guide where the received condition needs separate review.

Do not copy a tension, speed, pressure or drying setting from a small-roll trial into a wider trial merely because the material name is unchanged. The authorised line team must establish the relevant settings and safe operating envelope. This article provides no numerical machine settings.

4. Keep the comparison interpretable

A wider roll, a different adhesive and a new core surface introduced together can leave the team unable to identify what changed the result. Wherever the agreed trial permits, keep the reference construction and comparison conditions controlled. Record each intentional change and any unavoidable difference.

Comparable conditions do not always mean identical control-panel numbers. They mean that the responsible team understands how the actual process conditions relate to the reference. Note the contact side, bonding route, material revision, conditioning and inspection method rather than relying on a product name and a run date.

For PIR/PUR boards, the relevant question includes the foam-contact face and the defined foam/process combination. For gypsum boards, it includes facing position, slurry interaction and the condition after drying. For acoustic panels, the facing, bonding layer and finished-panel criteria need to remain distinguishable. Keep these as application-specific reviews rather than a universal “wider is better” test.

Use the controlled board-trial comparison guide to separate material and process changes.

For a PIR/PUR interface problem, use the dedicated facer-to-foam diagnostic article.

For gypsum-board conversion, keep the dedicated paper-to-glass-mat qualification review in scope.

5. Agree where evidence will be taken

Decide the observation positions before seeing the result. A practical draft sampling map can distinguish left, centre and right positions within the agreed usable zone, together with relevant positions along the run. The manufacturer’s quality team should set the actual number, location and timing of checks for the trial objective; this is not a fixed sampling standard.

Keep a board sample or observation linked to its source roll, orientation, run condition and position. Record the measurement method and the stage at which the result was judged. An appearance observation under one lighting condition and a bond observation after a different conditioning period should not silently become a single comparable acceptance result.

Where the objective concerns the finished board, evaluate the relevant finished construction. Loose-facer air permeability is not a substitute for panel sound-absorption evidence; a material report does not automatically cover a changed finished-board construction.

The lot and roll traceability guide explains how to keep material and trial records connected.

For acoustic evidence boundaries, distinguish facer airflow from panel absorption.

6. Write the hold points before the run

The useful result is a decision supported by a defined record, not simply “PASS” next to the supplier’s name. Separate permission to receive a roll, permission to run a trial and permission to consider the material for a later supply stage.

Finding Decision supported by the evidence What it does not establish
Identity, construction or contact face is unclear Hold release to the trial until the discrepancy is resolved That the material is defective
Coverage or equipment limits are unresolved Revise the request or trial scope before running That a larger nominal width is the correct fix
The roll runs, but comparison or sample records are incomplete Record a limited observation; agree what evidence is still needed Full-width qualification or repeatability
Defined trial criteria are met in the recorded construction Review the next evaluation or supplier-qualification step Universal compatibility, reserved capacity or commercial approval
An observation fails the agreed criterion Separate material, geometry and process evidence before deciding the next trial A proven root cause or a need to replace the supplier

Machine safety holds, quality acceptance and commercial decisions should remain separate. A technical discussion can continue while commercial availability is being confirmed, but neither should be described as an unconditional release.

Worked scenario: a good centre sample is not a width conclusion

Illustrative review scenario, not a GRECHO customer case: a manufacturer reviews a wider roll after a small sample has met an appearance criterion. The centre of the first board looks acceptable, so the initial note reads “surface approved.”

The proposed trial question, however, concerns acceptable facing over the full coverage zone. A centre observation does not show the left and right boundaries, the coating position relative to the board or the condition after the agreed converting step. The record is also incomplete if the board sample cannot be linked to the roll revision and run conditions.

The next action is not automatically to reject the material or request an even wider roll. First complete the agreed width and sample map. Distinguish a shortfall in usable coverage from a process-positioning issue and from a surface acceptance issue. The decision should state which question was answered and which remains open.

The lesson is about evidence coverage. No dimensions, machine settings, savings or customer outcomes are asserted in this scenario.

7. Leave the trial with a usable handover pack

Keep the material identity, drawing revision, agreed trial scope, actual conditions, observations, retained-sample references, exceptions and decision owner together. Record whether the next step is a revised sample, a repeat trial, document review or commercial discussion. Avoid a conclusion that is more general than the evidence.

For an initial GRECHO discussion, you do not need this complete pack. Start with the board application, target width and the question you want to resolve. Add the anticipated volume or trial window when known. We can then coordinate the material direction, sample discussion and available technical documents with selected supply-chain partners.

Share your application and width requirement through the Europe Wide-Width Facer Initiative.

A project discussion is not a confirmation of a particular production width, a commissioned line, reserved capacity or finished-board performance.

Questions before a wider-roll trial

Can a successful small-roll trial qualify a full-width roll?

Not by itself. It can support the material review, but full-width coverage, handling and across-width consistency need evidence for the proposed roll and board construction. State exactly which question the smaller trial answered.

Which settings should be used on the continuous line?

The authorised equipment and process owners should define them using the line’s procedures and the agreed material. This checklist does not prescribe tension, speed, nip pressure or drying conditions.

How should the trial distinguish nominal and usable width?

Use an annotated drawing that separates delivered width, accepted usable zones and required coverage. Identify the measurement stage and avoid counting an edge deduction twice. The width guide provides an illustrative dimensional model.

Does a request for a wider facer guarantee supply?

No. Feasibility, material construction, sample scope, timing and commercial terms need project-specific confirmation. Trial readiness and commercial availability are separate decisions.
